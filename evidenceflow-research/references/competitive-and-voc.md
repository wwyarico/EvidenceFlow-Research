# Competitive analysis and voice of customer

## Competitive study

Derive comparison dimensions from the decision, not from whichever product pages are easiest to read. Typical dimensions include target segment, job-to-be-done, workflow, capability, price and unit, onboarding, integration, privacy, evidence of performance, user friction, and switching cost.

For each matrix cell use one of:

- verified value with source ID;
- `NOT APPLICABLE` with reason;
- `UNKNOWN` when undocumented or unverified.

Never interpret undocumented as “No.” Separate advertised capability from tested performance. Preserve tier, geography, date, currency, and billing period for pricing.

### Opportunity construction

An opportunity combines an evidenced problem, an observed market gap, a plausible product response, a stated assumption or risk, and a way to validate it.

## Voice-of-customer study

Define the unit of analysis before coding: one respondent, review, answer, support ticket, or conversation turn. Retain a stable record ID and do not expose personal information.

### Codebook fields

`code, definition, include_when, exclude_when, example_record_ids, notes`

Start with a small provisional codebook, code a subset, revise definitions, then apply consistently. Allow multiple codes per record where justified. Preserve minority and contradictory cases.

### Counting and segments

Report counts and proportions only when sample construction and denominator are known. Phrase convenience-sample results as “mentioned in X of N analyzed records,” not as market prevalence. Compare segments only when attributes are available and cells are not misleadingly small; state the base size.

### Synthetic personas

Synthetic personas may brainstorm questions, edge cases, or hypotheses. Store outputs separately, label them `SYNTHETIC`, and exclude them from user counts, quotations, and findings.
