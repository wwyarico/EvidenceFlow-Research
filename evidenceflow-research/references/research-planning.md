# Research planning

## Convert the request into a decision question

A useful question contains a decision, alternatives, audience, market, and time window.

Weak: “Research AI meeting tools.”

Stronger: “Which shortlisted AI meeting assistant should a 20-person bilingual remote team pilot for three months, given transcription quality, language coverage, privacy, workflow integration, and total cost?”

## Planning fields

1. **Decision:** What will someone do differently after this research?
2. **Decision-maker:** Who owns the choice?
3. **Audience:** Who will consume the output?
4. **Scope:** Market, geography, user segment, date range, product tier.
5. **Alternatives:** Products or options included, plus inclusion rule.
6. **Criteria:** Dimensions that can change the decision.
7. **Evidence:** Available and required source types.
8. **Constraints:** Time, access, language, budget, privacy.
9. **Non-goals:** Questions the study will not answer.
10. **Output:** Memo, matrix, evidence file, presentation, or data table.

## Decompose the question

- **Descriptive:** What exists, for whom, at what price, with what capability?
- **Explanatory:** Why do users choose, reject, or struggle with it?
- **Decision:** Which option or opportunity best fits the constraints?

Avoid causal questions unless the design can support causality.

## Sampling

For products, define the shortlist rule before examining winners. For user material, record channel, period, language, user type, and inclusion/exclusion criteria. Do not mix reviews, interviews, surveys, and synthetic personas without labeling their different evidentiary weight.

## Stopping rule

Stop when all decision criteria have at least one suitable source and additional sources no longer change the comparison materially, or when the agreed time budget is reached. Report uncovered cells as gaps. Do not continue merely to make the bibliography longer.
